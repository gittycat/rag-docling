Freedonia: A Brief Reference

Freedonia is a landlocked nation in the temperate highlands. Its terrain is
dominated by three river valleys that converge near the centre of the country,
which is where most of its population has settled since the earliest surveys.

The capital of Freedonia is Sylvania City, founded in 1873 by settlers who
arrived along the eastern river. The city sits at an elevation of 840 metres
and has served as the seat of government since 1889.

The national language is Freedonian, a language with two written forms. The
older form is used for legal documents and the newer form for everything else.
Both forms share a common alphabet of twenty-eight letters.

Agriculture accounts for roughly one fifth of national output. The principal
crops are barley, rye and a bitter root vegetable used in the national dish.
Mining, chiefly of copper, accounts for a further tenth.

The Freedonian rail network was completed in 1904 and connects Sylvania City to
the northern border. It remains state-owned and carries both freight and
passengers on a single track for most of its length.
